The visual system

A visual system for learning to swim that fits any programme

PixtoLearn Swimming is a shared visual language for the water. Waterproof pictures show one clear action at a time, so instructors, families, and swimmers can teach and learn the same skills without relying on spoken instructions alone. It sits alongside the programme you already run: clearer sessions, stronger focus, and water safety built into the way you teach.

Inclusion

Works without shared speech or language. Pictures carry the lesson for non-verbal, neurodiverse, and multilingual swimmers.

Session structure

A visible order for the lesson. Transitions stay calm because everyone can see what comes next.

Swimmer focus

One picture, one action. Less verbal noise, clearer attention in the water.

Water safety

Float, tread, and self-rescue taught as real aquatic skills, not as a side note.

New skills

Break each skill into the steps that the learner needs, so new practice stays achievable.

Inside PixtoLearn

Three kinds of flashcard, and a clear session order

The system is built around different card types for teaching, motivating, and choosing, plus a First / Next / Then schedule so every swimmer can see the flow of the lesson.

Main teaching card

Break It Down

Two sides. The front shows the goal. The reverse breaks it into the steps that skill needs: three, four, or more.

Toys and equipment

Single Objects

One toy or piece of equipment per card. Use them as the activity, as a motivator, or inside a visual schedule.

Break and choose

Break and Choose

One side signals break time. The other lets the swimmer choose a preferred toy, game, or activity.

Break It Down in action

Session order

First. Next. Then.

Stands hold the visual schedule upright so every swimmer can see what comes first, what comes next, and what comes then.

  1. First A familiar or focus activity
  2. Next The new skill or practice
  3. Then A motivator, game, or break

Real world implementation

The same cards. Real water. Real lives.

When swim lessons are scarce and language gets in the way, a waterproof picture still teaches what to do next.

Why this matters

Water safety cannot wait for perfect conditions

In many communities near open water, children learn to swim with limited coaches, mixed languages, and almost no waterproof teaching tools.

PixtoLearn takes the same visual system used in UK pools into those sessions: clear actions, calm steps, skills that travel.

Already in use from Kenya to Colombia, and in coastal programmes where drowning risk is highest.
