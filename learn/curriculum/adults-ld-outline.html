Visual Independence for Adults with Learning Disabilities

Practical visual systems for daily living, community participation and supported independence. Designed for support workers, families, day services and adult social care teams.

Level: Niche / professional + family
Modules: 7
Duration: about 2.5 to 3 hours
Audience: support workers, day centres, supported living, families, community teams
Products: Routine cards / custom sequences + App Focus
Suggested prerequisite: Visual Teaching Foundations
Tone: Adult dignity first. No childish framing. Age-appropriate visuals.

Module map

  1. 1. Independence through clarity (adult framing)
  2. 2. Mapping the day: priorities, not clutter
  3. 3. Self-care and domestic routines
  4. 4. Community and travel sequences
  5. 5. Choice, consent and dignified prompting
  6. 6. Team consistency in adult services
  7. 7. Practice lab: one adult, one independence plan

Module 1: Independence through clarity (adult framing)

About 20 minutes

Objective: position visual supports as tools for adult autonomy, not childish control.

OpenIndependence grows when steps are clear, predictable and respectful.
See itAdult kitchen or flat: verbal nagging vs adult-style sequence strip.
Get itDignity; competence; visuals as access; avoid infantilising design.
Try itSort examples: respectful adult visual vs childish / controlling visual.
Case itStaff uses cartoon baby icons for a 34-year-old; person refuses the board.
Take itAudit: one current visual. Make it more adult (language, imagery, placement).
CheckWhat makes a visual support adult-appropriate.
Video (60-75s) Supported living kitchen: adult follows a clear photo sequence for making a simple meal. Subtitles. Calm, respectful framing.

Module 2: Mapping the day: priorities, not clutter

About 20-25 minutes

Objective: build a day map with a few high-impact routines instead of covering every wall.

OpenToo many visuals create the same overload as too many words.
See itOverloaded flat vs 2-3 key routine stations (morning, leaving, evening).
Get itPick friction points; one sequence per friction; finished matters.
Try itFrom a sample day list, choose the 3 routines worth visualising first.
Case itDay centre adds 40 cards overnight; attendance anxiety rises.
Take itDay map worksheet: morning / leave / return / evening (pick 2 to start).
CheckBest starting number of routines.
Video (60s) Before/after of a hallway or kitchen with reduced visual clutter and one clear strip.

Module 3: Self-care and domestic routines

About 25 minutes

Objective: design short visual sequences for hygiene, dressing, laundry or meals with minimal verbal load.

OpenDomestic skills stick when steps stay visible and consistent across staff.
See itHandwashing, tea-making or laundry sequence in real home/day centre.
Get it4-6 steps; photo realism; prompt fade; privacy for personal care.
Try itBuild a 5-step sequence for a chosen domestic skill.
Case itDifferent support workers teach toothbrushing three different ways.
Take itOne domestic sequence ready to print/place + staff cue card.
CheckWhy shared sequences beat verbal-only coaching.
Video (75s) Adult completes a domestic routine with sequence cards; support worker points once, waits, celebrates finished.

Module 4: Community and travel sequences

About 25 minutes

Objective: support safer community access with portable visuals (cards + App).

OpenCommunity anxiety often comes from unknown next steps.
See itBus/shop trip preview on App or keyring cards; then real transition.
Get itPreview; first-then for travel; help/break request; portable tools.
Try itMatch community challenge to visual tool (preview / sequence / help card).
Case itOuting cancelled mid-way with no visual explanation; distress escalates.
Take itCommunity outing plan: preview + 4 steps + finished + help.
CheckBest use of App vs physical cards outdoors.
Video (75s) App Focus preview at home, then keyring/cards used at a door or stop. No identifiable public faces without consent.

Module 5: Choice, consent and dignified prompting

About 20-25 minutes

Objective: use visuals to offer real choice and reduce controlling prompts.

OpenIndependence includes choosing, refusing and asking for help.
See itChoice board for activity options; wait time after offering.
Get itMeaningful choice; least intrusive prompt; never remove visuals as punishment.
Try itRank prompts from least to most intrusive for an adult learner.
Case itStaff physically moves adult through steps while talking constantly.
Take itPrompt plan + choice moments for one routine.
CheckWhat counts as dignified prompting.
Video (60s) Support worker offers two activity cards, waits, follows the adult's choice. Short subtitle on wait time.

Module 6: Team consistency in adult services

About 20 minutes

Objective: make the visual system survive shift changes and multiple agencies.

OpenIf every shift reinvents the steps, independence resets daily.
See itHandover: photo of strip + App copy + one-page agreement.
Get itSingle source of truth; who can edit; how bank/agency staff learn it.
Try itSpot inconsistencies across three staff descriptions of the same routine.
Case itNight staff ignore morning sequence; morning fails.
Take itTeam agreement template for one adult.
CheckWhat belongs in a shared system handover.
Video (60-75s) Quick handover moment: staff shows incoming colleague the sequence station and App backup.

Module 7: Practice lab: one adult, one independence plan

About 30-35 minutes

Objective: produce a usable independence plan for one adult you support.

OpenCapstone: apply Modules 1-6 to one real person (anonymised).
See itWorked example plan: day map, 2 routines, community preview, team agreement.
Get itQuality bar: adult tone, short steps, portable option, staff consistency.
Try itGuided plan builder form.
Case itFix a weak plan (too many steps, childish icons, no finished, no team share).
Take itDownload plan + next product actions (cards/App).
CheckSelf-rubric with 6 criteria.
Video (90s) Screencast building the plan + photo of finished adult sequence in situ.

Production notes

  • Age-appropriate photography and language throughout.
  • Strong link to day services / supported living org licences.
  • App Focus is especially useful for community previews.
  • Cross-link premium neurodiverse course for deeper communication/sensory content.
