Visual Support for Neurodiverse and Non-Verbal Learners

Gold-standard module outline for PixtoLearn Academy's future premium course. Covers autism, learning disabilities, limited verbal communication and sensory needs across home, school, therapy, pool and community.

Level: Premium / Practitioner pathway candidate
Modules: 9 + optional certification lab
Duration: about 3.5 to 4.5 hours
Audience: families, educators, therapists, instructors, support staff, organisations
Products: Packs, WOW cards, App
Suggested prerequisite: Visual Teaching Foundations (launch course 1 in global order)
Relation to Swimming M4: Swimming Module 4 stays a short aquatic application. This course is the full cross-setting system.

Module map

  1. 1. Understanding neurodiversity and non-verbal communication
  2. 2. Sensory load and environment design
  3. 3. Core visual systems that reduce uncertainty
  4. 4. AAC-friendly visuals and communication pairing
  5. 5. Regulation first: calm before teaching
  6. 6. Teaching skills without relying on speech
  7. 7. Behaviour as communication
  8. 8. Consistency across people and settings
  9. 9. Practice lab: one learner, one visual plan
  10. 10. Optional: Practitioner portfolio

Module 1: Understanding neurodiversity and non-verbal communication

About 20-25 minutes

Objective: explain neurodiversity and non-verbal communication in practical teaching terms, without deficit-only language.

OpenWhat this course will help you do with one learner this month. Clarify: non-verbal does not mean non-communicating.
See itSide-by-side: adult talks at learner vs adult uses short cue + visual; learner engagement differs.
Get itThree ideas: processing differences; communication beyond speech; visuals as access, not reward.
Try itSort statements into helpful / unhelpful assumptions about non-verbal learners.
Case itStaff says "he understands everything if he wants to" after a meltdown during verbal instructions.
Take itOne-page learner snapshot template: strengths, sensory notes, communication preferences.
CheckMCQ: best definition of visual support for non-verbal learners.
Video (60-75s) Real or carefully consented session: learner looks away during long talk; then engages when shown a clear next card. Subtitles. No diagnostic labelling on screen.

Module 2: Sensory load and environment design

About 20-25 minutes

Objective: spot sensory overload sources and reduce them before adding more teaching demand.

OpenIf the environment is too loud or busy, no visual system will land well.
See itBusy wall / noisy room vs stripped focus strip with one sequence visible.
Get itLoad sources (noise, light, proximity, clutter); reduce before teach; one visual focus.
Try itAudit photo: mark 5 overload risks and 3 quick fixes.
Case itPool or classroom: learner covers ears; adult increases verbal volume.
Take itEnvironment checklist for your main setting (home / school / pool).
CheckWhat to do first when sensory overload is clear.
Video (60-75s) Camera pans a cluttered support space, then the same space with one sequence strip and quieter background. Optional soft ambient sound contrast (careful with accessibility: offer mute-friendly cut).

Module 3: Core visual systems that reduce uncertainty

About 25 minutes

Objective: choose and build first-then, schedules, sequences and finished cues for one daily context.

OpenUncertainty drives anxiety. Visual systems make the next step knowable.
See itBuild a first-then and a 4-step sequence with PixtoLearn-style cards.
Get itWhen to use schedule vs sequence vs first-then; keep finished meaningful.
Try itMatch learner need to system type (transition, skill, full morning).
Case itFamily uses 20 cards for leaving the house; child freezes.
Take itBuild one first-then and one 4-step sequence for this week.
CheckBest system for a short transition into an activity.
Video (75s) Hands build first-then, then a short sequence; cut to learner following it at a door or table. Product cards visible but not salesy.

Module 4: AAC-friendly visuals and communication pairing

About 20-25 minutes

Objective: pair visual teaching cards with existing communication methods without replacing speech therapy advice.

OpenVisual teaching supports access to tasks. AAC supports expressive communication. They work together.
See itAdult models pointing to a sequence card and offers a choice board / AAC opportunity.
Get itDo not block AAC; keep vocabulary consistent; wait after modelling.
Try itSort: teaching sequence vs communication choice board vs reward chart.
Case itStaff removes AAC device "so they focus on the cards".
Take itPairing plan: task sequence + how the learner can request help/break.
CheckCorrect relationship between visual teaching and AAC.
Video (60-75s) Consented therapy/home clip: sequence for a task + learner uses device or choice cards to request "help" or "finished". On-screen note: follow the learner's AAC plan.

Module 5: Regulation first: calm before teaching

About 25 minutes

Objective: prioritise co-regulation and known routines when a learner is dysregulated, then return to teaching.

OpenNew skills cannot land on a nervous system in survival mode.
See itEscalation when demands continue vs return to known visual routine.
Get itReduce demand; use known visuals; co-regulate; re-enter teaching later.
Try itOrder the regulation ladder for a rising escalation.
Case itPool or classroom: learner hits / withdraws mid skill; adult pushes to "finish the set".
Take itPersonal regulation plan: early signs + known calming visual routine.
CheckWhat to prioritise when dysregulated.
Video (75-90s) Two endings of the same moment (acted carefully or real with consent): push through vs return to "finished / calm" visual. Prefer real calm recovery if available.

Module 6: Teaching skills without relying on speech

About 25 minutes

Objective: break a skill into visual steps and teach it with minimal verbal load.

OpenIf the learner cannot use speech easily, the teaching channel must not depend on speech.
See itSkill demo (coat, handwashing, pool step, tidy-up) taught with 4 cards and almost no talk.
Get itTask analysis; one idea per card; model-point-wait; fade prompts.
Try itBuild a 4-step sequence for a chosen skill (sequence builder).
Case itInstructor narrates every micro-movement while learner looks overwhelmed.
Take itSkill sheet: name, 4 steps, prompt plan.
CheckBest teaching pattern for low verbal load.
Video (75s) Silent-leaning teach: adult points to cards, models once, learner attempts. Subtitles carry the method voiceover (short).

Module 7: Behaviour as communication

About 25 minutes

Objective: read common behaviours as communication and use visuals to prevent and respond without shame.

OpenBehaviour often says: too much, too fast, unclear, or no way to request a break.
See itRefusal / running / shutdown; then same setting with clearer visual path and break card.
Get itPossible messages behind behaviour; preventive visuals; never use visuals as punishment.
Try itMatch behaviour moment to likely need and visual response.
Case itTeam removes preferred visuals after "bad behaviour".
Take itPrevention card: early signs + break/help visuals available.
CheckWhy visuals must not be used as punishment.
Video (60-75s) Focus on prevention: help/break card introduced before escalation. Avoid sensational meltdown footage.

Module 8: Consistency across people and settings

About 20-25 minutes

Objective: design one shared visual language across home, school, therapy and pool.

OpenInconsistency between adults recreates uncertainty.
See itSame sequence used at home door and poolside / classroom.
Get itShared symbols; who updates; handover photo of the strip; App as portable copy.
Try itSpot inconsistencies in a 3-adult scenario.
Case itMum, TA and instructor each use different steps for the same transition.
Take itOne-page shared system agreement for your learner.
CheckWhat makes a system (not a one-off).
Video (75-90s) Mini tour: same visual language in two settings. End card: Packs for place-based use, App for portable Focus.

Production notes

  • All modules use the gold shell: Open, See it, Get it, Try it, Case it, Take it, Check.
  • Videos: 60-90s, subtitled, consented, dignity-first. Avoid meltdown spectacle.
  • Language: English (en-GB) first for worldwide sale; plain and respectful.
  • Upsell path: Foundations ? this premium course ? Packs/App ? Practitioner certificate.
  • Do not duplicate Swimming Module 4; cross-link it as an aquatic application example inside Modules 6 and 8.
