Using waterproof cards in a live lesson

Learning objective Place, pace and prompt with waterproof visual cards so they support teaching without becoming a distraction.

Placement matters

Cards work when the learner can see them at the moment of action. Common options:

  • Pool edge in reading order (left to right, or your agreed direction)
  • On a waterproof board or stand within the learner's line of sight
  • Held briefly by the instructor, then returned to the sequence

Keep the active card clearer than the rest (slightly forward, highlighted, or pointed to) so attention stays on the current step.

Pacing and prompting

Point to the card, give a short spoken cue if needed, then allow time to try. Avoid stacking long verbal instructions on top of the visual.

If the learner is stuck, prompt toward the card first ("Look at the next picture") before adding more words. Fade prompts as independence grows.

Keep the lesson flow calm

Prepare your sequence before learners enter the water. Change cards between attempts, not mid-movement, unless safety requires it. End with a clear "finished" or cool-down visual so the session closes predictably.
