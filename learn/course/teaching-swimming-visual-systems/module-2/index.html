Breaking a swim skill into clear steps

Learning objective Break one swimming skill into a short visual sequence that a learner can follow without overloaded verbal instructions.

One skill, a few clear steps

Many swim skills look simple to an instructor but feel complex in the water. A strong visual sequence shows only the steps that matter for that skill, in order, with no extra noise.

  • Choose one skill (for example: push and glide, or face in the water)
  • List the essential steps a learner must do in order
  • Remove optional detail that can wait for a later session
  • Match each step to one clear image or card

Good sequence design

Aim for 3 to 6 steps for most beginner skills. If you need more, split the skill into two teaching blocks. Each card should answer: "What do I do now?"

Use consistent layout and language across cards so learners recognise the system, not only individual pictures.

Teach the sequence before the water work

Show the sequence on the side first when you can. Point to the first card, model the step, then invite the learner to try. Move to the next card only when the current step is understood enough to practise safely.

Activity - Check your understanding

Choose true or false, then check.

A visual sequence should include every possible detail of a swimming skill in one go.
